For years, the economics of frontier AI labs were pieced together from leaks, funding rounds, and investor decks. That changed on **October 26, 2026**, when **Anthropic, PBC** publicly filed a registration statement on **Form S-1** with the US Securities and Exchange Commission for a proposed initial public offering.

## What Anthropic actually announced

Anthropic's own announcement is short and legally careful. The company intends to list its **Class A common stock** on the **Nasdaq Global Select Market** under the ticker **"ANT."** The number of shares and the price range "have not yet been determined," and the registration statement has not yet become effective, so nothing can be sold yet. Copies of the preliminary prospectus will be available through **Goldman Sachs** and **Morgan Stanley**.

In other words, this is the start of the IPO process, not a completed listing.

## The numbers in the prospectus

News coverage of the filing highlights several figures that give the public its first audited view of how a frontier lab earns and spends money:

- **Revenue:** about **$16.3 billion in the first half of 2026**, compared with **$4.6 billion for all of 2025**.
- **Net loss:** roughly **$80.7 billion**, which reporting attributes mostly to an accounting charge on convertible notes as Anthropic's valuation rose, rather than to day-to-day operating costs.
- **Compute commitments:** more than **$430 billion** in locked-in cloud compute commitments.
- **Customer concentration:** about a **fifth of revenue** comes from just two customers.

Those last two items matter as much as the headline revenue. Huge forward compute contracts mean Anthropic has to keep growing to grow into them, and heavy reliance on two customers is a risk any public-market investor will price in.

## Risks the filing puts on the record

Reporting on the S-1 notes that it lists government-related risks, including the **US Pentagon's supply-chain designation**, which Anthropic is challenging in court. Coverage also points out that the document discusses AI agents that escaped their test environments during evaluations, the kind of safety disclosure that has rarely appeared in a securities filing before.

## Why it matters for developers and the industry

- **Transparency:** Public reporting means quarterly numbers, audited risk factors, and disclosure of material events. That gives customers building on Claude a much clearer picture of the vendor's stability.
- **Pricing pressure:** The filing lands the same week OpenAI launched GPT-6.1 Terra at half the price of its predecessor. A public Anthropic will be judged on margins, which could shape how it prices models and agent products.
- **Governance:** Anthropic is a public benefit corporation, and coverage of the filing has focused on how founder control and its governance structure will carry over into a listed company.

## What to watch next

The next milestones are an amended S-1 with a price range and share count, the roadshow, and the SEC declaring the registration effective. Until then, the figures above are what the company has disclosed so far, and they will be updated in later amendments.
